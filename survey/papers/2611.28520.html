arXiv ↗ PDF ↗ ✅ Core · Relevance 5/5

Abstract

Video generation models are widely used as 3D priors for novel view synthesis and 3D scene generation. Fine-tuned for perception, generation models also excel at dense prediction such as depth estimation. However, methods that apply video generation models to 3D reconstruction either predict depth without cameras or generate geometry and estimate cameras through many iterative denoising steps, which makes them slower. In this paper, we present $\textbf{VG3R}$, which turns a pretrained video generation model into a feed-forward 3D reconstruction model. Our key observation is that such a model already captures the depth and camera motion of a video, but not in the form required for reconstruction. We introduce two components: $\textit{clip-level shift prediction}$, which corrects depth distortion caused by the unknown shift in decoded inverse depth, and a $\textit{multi-layer camera head}$, which predicts per-frame cameras parameters by combining features across transformer blocks. Using Wan2.1 as its video backbone, VG3R reconstructs a scene and estimate the camera of every frame without iterative denoising. It achieves the lowest ATE and reconstruction errors among methods built on video generation models, runs about 4 and 14 times faster than Aether and Geo4D. VG3R also demonstrates competitive depth estimation and 3D reconstruction performance compared with geometry foundation models. Project page: https://github.com/KTH-RPL/VG3R

Reason
VG3R directly predicts depth and per-frame camera parameters from unposed video frames in a feed-forward manner, which is a comparable dense scene geometry prediction from unposed images as required for core. It is not a DUSt3R extension but qualifies under the broader core criterion of directly predicting reconstructable scene representations from unposed inputs.
